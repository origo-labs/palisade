# Corpus

The measurement corpus for PRD §9 criterion 3: *does a **generated** contract
find real problems in real agent-authored changes, at an acceptable
false-positive rate?*

## The rule this corpus exists to keep

**A finding is a false positive only if a human says the code was fine.** There
is no ground-truth label, and that is a feature: a label would have to come
from the same people who wrote the gates, and the whole measurement would
regress to the circularity `EVIDENCE.md` §6 got wrong.

So the number is produced by reading findings and deciding, per finding, whether
a reviewer would call it noise. A `warn` finding on an `accept` verdict is
exactly the case worth judging.

## Two rules the harness is built on

1. **The contract is generated by `palisade init`, not written here.** A
   hand-written contract in the harness would be the person running the
   measurement choosing gates they already know work.
2. **Commits are pinned.** A corpus measured against `main` in one month and
   re-measured in another is not reproducible. The pins below are the corpus.

## What is not here, and why

**The user's own six repositories** (`warmplane`, `pearls`, `rust-okf`,
`duiker`, `semble-rs`, `gliner2-candle`) are a separate group. They are
agent-written and have real merge history — 137 merges, which is where the
`tests_not_deleted` false positive was found. They are **not** criterion 5
evidence: one author, who also chooses the gates and judges the findings. A
false positive on our own corpus is a bug report; on someone else's project it
is why they turn it off.

## Known limits of this corpus

- **`proton-tui` has 3 usable merges and `simonw/tools` has 2.** A
  false-positive *rate* from those is not a rate. They are contract-quality
  evidence — "did `init` miss a rule the project wrote down" — and nothing
  more.
- **`adk-rust` is large** (508k LOC, 1709 files). `checks_green` on it is
  minutes per merge, so the FP corpus either samples merges or calibrates
  `checks_green` separately. The other eight gates are per-diff and cheap.
- **Agent-authorship is asserted in prose**, not proven. `proton-tui` states it
  plainly; `adk-rust` and `awaken` ship agent configuration files, which shows
  agents are in the loop without quantifying how much of the code they wrote.
  The corpus records the evidence rather than assuming it.
- **Both dated repos are recent** (2026). There is no multi-year agent-written
  history to draw on, which is itself a finding about the population.
